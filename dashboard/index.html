Accessibility findings from real applicants

When someone applies for a job using BRIDGE, the extension records the accessibility barriers it found on that form. Their reports show here: what each form is doing to the people trying to use it, a suggested fix for each barrier, and what has changed between one report and the next.

These are automated WCAG 2.2 A/AA accessibility findings. They are not a conformance decision and not a legal opinion. BRIDGE does not certify WCAG compliance and does not replace testing with real assistive technology. Human review is required for a WCAG conformance claim.

What this is

The customer is the platform, not the employer

Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and their competitors host thousands of employers' application forms on one set of components. When one of those components locks out a screen-reader user, it locks them out everywhere it is used — and when the vendor fixes it once, it is fixed for every employer on the platform. That is who we sell to.

Findings from the people actually blocked

A form that passed an accessibility review does not stay accessible. Employers add new job postings constantly, each one new human-written content poured into a fixed template — new screening questions, new embedded assessments, new third-party widgets. The vendor ships platform updates on its own schedule. A redesigned date picker or upload widget can break keyboard access weeks after the last audit passed.

What arrives here is not a crawl. It is a report from a real applicant, on the real form they were trying to complete, at the moment they were trying to complete it — including the steps a crawler never reaches, because a person filled in what was needed to get there. An audit is a photograph. This is what the form did to someone.

What the vendor gets

  • Findings from the forms their customers actually publish, on the journeys applicants actually take.
  • A comparison against the last report for the same form, so a barrier that was not there before stands out.
  • Evidence that reflects the product as it is now, in the plain language of what an applicant could not do.

What it costs

A subscription, per platform, for as long as reports keep arriving. No per-seat charge and no setup fee. The vendor runs nothing and installs nothing.

The figure belongs on the pitch slide, where it can be defended, not in this dashboard. Nothing on this page is a number we did not measure.

What this demo does not do

No accounts, no billing, no automated crawling. Sending a report from the extension to this page is not built yet: the report shown is one real BRIDGE scan of the Acme Careers test form, captured ahead of time. BRIDGE never scans a form on an employer's behalf, never signs in anywhere, and never submits an application on its own.
