Application form monitoring

BRIDGE scans your customers' live application forms and tells you what changed since the last scan: barriers they fixed, barriers still open, and barriers that are new. The new ones are the alert.

These are automated WCAG 2.2 A/AA accessibility findings. They are not a conformance decision and not a legal opinion. BRIDGE does not certify WCAG compliance and does not replace testing with real assistive technology. Human review is required for a WCAG conformance claim.

What this is

The customer is the platform, not the employer

Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and their competitors host thousands of employers' application forms on one set of components. When one of those components locks out a screen-reader user, it locks them out everywhere it is used — and when the vendor fixes it once, it is fixed for every employer on the platform. That is who we sell to.

Monitoring, not a one-off audit

A form that passed an accessibility review does not stay accessible. Two things keep changing underneath it. Employers add new job postings constantly, each one new human-written content poured into a fixed template — new screening questions, new embedded assessments, new third-party widgets. And the vendor ships platform updates on its own schedule, outside any individual employer's control. A redesigned date picker or upload widget can break keyboard access weeks after the last audit passed.

An audit is a photograph. What a vendor needs is a smoke alarm: something watching every form on the platform that says this one broke on Tuesday.

What the vendor gets

  • Continuous scanning of their customers' live forms. The vendor runs nothing.
  • An alert the moment a scan finds a barrier the previous scan did not.
  • Current accessibility evidence for procurement, which asks for proof that reflects the product as it is now, not as it was at the last review.

Pricing

Priced per monitored form, per month, on the number of forms the vendor puts under monitoring. No per-seat charge and no setup fee: the vendor hands over posting URLs, we scan them on a schedule.

The figure belongs on the pitch slide, where it can be defended, not in this dashboard. Nothing on this page is a number we did not measure.

What this demo does not do

No accounts, no billing, no scheduler. The monitor is run by hand here; in production it runs on a schedule. The monitor only ever reads a page — it never fills a field, never clicks forward and never submits an application.
